There are three LAWS in AI.

1. The Law of the Dumb Zone Effect

AI models have an optimal context window. Providing too much information (crossing the threshold, often around 96k tokens) can cause the AI's performance to degrade, pushing it into a “garbage zone” where it becomes less effective.

2. The Clustering Law

AI models process information by triggering specific parts of their “brain” or internal clusters. Giving too many instructions or rules at once triggers too many clusters, leading to scattered, non-deterministic outputs. It is better to prompt in a sequential, focused manner.

3. U-Shaped Attention

Models tend to prioritize the first and last tokens in the context window, while often losing focus on information in the middle. To optimize results, place system prompts at the end and keep your critical instructions at the very beginning of the query.
